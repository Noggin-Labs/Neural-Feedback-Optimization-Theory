# NFOT v7 literature review

The verified v6 review covered official bigP3BCI documentation, BCI feedback timing, eligibility traces and three-factor learning, neuroadaptive education, repeated-measures validation, causal feedback-learning experiments, and physical display timing. It is preserved in `research/v6_sources/08-synthesis.md`.

Across these domains, feedback timing is consequential but conditional. P300-speller and restorative BCI studies show timing-related effects in particular paradigms, but do not establish a universal optimum. Synaptic studies support transient eligibility states gated by later monoamines in restricted ex vivo preparations, but do not show that a behavioral BCI interval is a synaptic trace. Neuroadaptive education studies demonstrate feasible adaptive timing architectures, not a general latency rule or durable learning benefit. Delayed-feedback learning effects vary by task, outcome, spacing, and context.

Methods literature supports mixed or clustered repeated-measures analysis, participant-grouped validation for unseen-person claims, and nested model selection when tuning is performed. Timing studies show that software/API timestamps can differ from physical display transitions because of hardware, scan-out, pixel response, driver, browser, and measurement-threshold effects.

The literature therefore supports NFOT as a testable measurement and experimental-design question. It does not establish novelty, a universal timing law, a biological mechanism, or a causal effect in the current dataset.
