# NFOT v7 red-team review

The strongest proposed claim is that the Write-Back Gap, especially its distribution, contains information about performance beyond ordinary latency and may reflect a learning-relevant timing process.

The most serious objection is that the entire association could be a measurement or difficulty artifact. Longer gaps may simply indicate more flashes, harder selections, fatigue, task state, or interface/processing delays. The raw source data are missing, so this criticism cannot currently be answered. A second objection is leakage or overfitting: the historical thresholds, LOSO and AUC claims lack the exact fold outputs and tuning record. A third objection is causal category error: feedback after a response cannot cause that response's correctness. A fourth is that a conventional latency model may explain everything NFOT explains. A fifth is biological overreach: ex vivo eligibility traces do not make a behavioral timestamp a synaptic measurement.

The current evidence answers only partially. The derived table is arithmetically coherent, the guarded code fails closed on several invalid inputs, and the literature supports prospective timing experiments. None resolves source semantics, leakage, causal effect, added predictive value, or mechanism.

The theory should be narrowed or abandoned if source reconstruction changes the rows materially, the association disappears after prespecified difficulty/order/session controls, grouped prediction does not beat a conventional latency baseline, or randomized delay fails to affect the prespecified downstream outcome. A positive result would still support only the tested scope.
