# NFOT v7 neuroscience mechanism review

## Synaptic eligibility traces

Ex vivo cortical, hippocampal, and corticostriatal studies show that prior activity can create a transient state later gated by a neuromodulatory signal. Some tested effects occur over seconds; the window, receptor dependence, and direction vary by preparation. This supports biological plausibility for delayed credit assignment, not equivalence between `L` and a synaptic trace.

## Three-factor learning and neuromodulation

A three-factor account combines local activity, a modulatory signal, and synaptic change. The reviewed literature supports this architecture in restricted preparations. NFOT has not measured dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, eligibility, synaptic change, or neural plasticity. Behavioral event timestamps alone cannot establish these mechanisms.

## Temporal credit assignment

Temporal-difference and reinforcement-learning frameworks already explain why consequences can influence earlier activity through eligibility or value-estimation mechanisms. NFOT would need a distinct quantitative prediction or useful measurement advantage over these frameworks to claim theoretical novelty.

## Feedback timing and behavior

Human learning studies show conditional effects of immediate and delayed feedback on later outcomes. These results support prospective timing manipulation but do not imply a single direction or mechanism.

## What additional evidence would be required

A biological claim would require concurrent neural/physiological measurements, verified physical timing, a prespecified timing manipulation, and a model linking observed signals to the proposed mechanism. Until then, NFOT should use “system timing variable” or “candidate temporal predictor,” not “eligibility trace.”
